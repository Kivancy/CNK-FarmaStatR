# EMA %90 güven aralığı: OIP Rev. 2, Bölüm 5.1 ölçüt 8

## 1. Soru ve kapsam
Test (T) ve referans (R) ürününün in vitro ölçümünün (ör. aerodinamik partikül boyutu dağılımında bir basamak grubunun kütlesi) geometrik ortalamaları oranı 0,85–1,18 içinde mi? EMA’nın inhalasyon ürünleri kılavuzu (CPMP/EWP/4151/00 Rev. 2, 2025) Bölüm 5.1 ölçüt 8’e göre karşılaştırma, log-normal dağılım varsayımıyla bu oranın %90 güven aralığı (GA) olarak sunulur. Kılavuz, %90 GA’nın seri ve yaşam evresi yapısıyla nasıl hesaplanacağını tanımlamaz; platform üç aday yolu birlikte gösterir (KK-27). Hesap simülasyon motorunun validasyonlu fonksiyonlarıyla yapılır (`invitro_array`, `ema_ci_fast`).

## 2. Model ve gösterim
Her ürün için ℓ seri, seri başına n birim ve birim başına m yaşam evresi ölçülür (dengeli tasarım). $X_{ijs}$: j serisindeki i biriminin s evresindeki log ölçümü. Veri üretme modeli FSR-TN-PBE-01’dekidir: sabit evre etkisi, rastgele seri ve birim etkileri, birim içi hata.

- $\bar U_{ij}$: birimin m evre ortalaması; $\bar B_j$: serinin birim ortalamalarının ortalaması; $\bar g$: ürün ortalaması.
- $\hat d = \bar g_T - \bar g_R$; nokta tahmini $\mathrm{GMR} = e^{\hat d}$.
- $v_k$ ve $\nu_k$: k ürününde ortalamanın varyansı ve serbestlik derecesi; üç yol bu ikisini farklı tahmin eder.

## 3. Denklemler
Ortalamanın varyansı ve serbestlik derecesi, üç yol (k = T, R; ürün indisi yazılmamıştır):

$$ s_U^2 = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{n} (\bar U_{ij} - \bar g)^2}{\ell n - 1}, \qquad v^{(a)} = \frac{s_U^2}{\ell n}, \qquad \nu^{(a)} = \ell n - 1 $$ :: FarmaStatR türetimi (KK-27; FSR-TN-PBE-01 Bölüm 5.3, yol a)

Gerçek modelde ($\sigma^2_B$ seri, $\sigma^2_C$ kap, $\sigma^2_W$ kap içi varyans) ortalamanın varyansı $\operatorname{Var}(\bar g) = \sigma^2_B/\ell + \sigma^2_C/(\ell n) + \sigma^2_W/(\ell n m)$’dir. (a)’nın beklentisi ise seri terimini yalnız $(\ell-1)/(\ell n - 1)$ oranında içerir (3 × 10’da 2/29); seri varyansı varken aralık bu yüzden dar çıkar:

$$ E\left[v^{(a)}\right] = \frac{1}{\ell n}\left(\sigma^2_C + \frac{\sigma^2_W}{m} + \sigma^2_B\,\frac{n(\ell-1)}{\ell n - 1}\right) $$ :: Searle, Casella ve McCulloch (2006) §3.5a, Denk. (50)–(51), Tablo 3.3’ten türetildi (FarmaStatR türetimi)

$$ s_B^2 = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} (\bar B_j - \bar g)^2}{\ell - 1}, \qquad v^{(b)} = \frac{s_B^2}{\ell}, \qquad \nu^{(b)} = \ell - 1 $$ :: FarmaStatR türetimi (KK-27; FSR-TN-PBE-01 Bölüm 5.3, yol b); dağılım: (ℓ − 1)v^(b)/Var(ḡ) ~ χ²(ℓ − 1), Casella ve Berger (2002) Teorem 5.3.1(c); dengeli tasarımda PROC MIXED NOBOUND ya da METHOD=TYPE3 ile aynı (Stroup ve ark. 2018 §5.7.2; SAS kontrolü, FSR-LOG-01 G-105)

Yol (c), seri ve birimi rastgele etkili karma model (REML). Dengeli tasarımda üç tabakanın kareler ortalaması:

$$ MS_{\mathrm{seri}} = \frac{n m \sum_j (\bar B_j - \bar g)^2}{\ell - 1}, \qquad MS_{\mathrm{birim}} = \frac{m \sum_j \sum_i (\bar U_{ij} - \bar B_j)^2}{\ell (n - 1)} $$ :: Searle, Casella ve McCulloch (2006) Bölüm 4 (dengeli tasarım)
$$ MS_{\mathrm{art}} = \frac{\sum_j \sum_i \sum_s (X_{ijs} - \bar U_{ij} - \bar S_s + \bar g)^2}{(\ell n - 1)(m - 1)} $$ :: Searle, Casella ve McCulloch (2006) Bölüm 4 (dengeli tasarım)
$$ (\hat\theta_1, \hat\theta_2, \hat\theta_3) = \underset{\theta_1 \ge \theta_2 \ge \theta_3}{\mathrm{arg\,min}} \sum_{t=1}^{3} \mathrm{sd}_t\,(MS_t - \theta_t)^2, \qquad v^{(c)} = \frac{\hat\theta_1}{\ell n m} $$ :: Searle, Casella ve McCulloch (2006) Bölüm 3.8 (dengeli veride REML, sınırlı çözüm); üç tabakaya genişletme: FarmaStatR türetimi
$$ \nu^{(c)} = \text{seri tabakasıyla havuzlanan tabakaların serbestlik dereceleri toplamı} $$ :: FarmaStatR türetimi (FSR-TN-PBE-01 Bölüm 5.3); m = 1’de PROC MIXED sınırlı REML + DDFM=SATTERTHWAITE ile aynı (SAS kontrolü, FSR-LOG-01 G-105)

Havuzlama olmazsa $v^{(c)} = MS_{\mathrm{seri}}/(\ell n m) = v^{(b)}$ ve $\nu^{(c)} = \ell - 1$; (c) yolu (b) ile aynıdır. $\bar S_s$ evre ortalamasıdır; m = 1 ise artık tabakası yoktur. Güven aralığı (her yol için):

$$ SE = \sqrt{v_T + v_R}, \qquad \nu = \frac{(v_T + v_R)^2}{v_T^2/\nu_T + v_R^2/\nu_R} $$ :: Satterthwaite (1946); Casella ve Berger (2002) Örnek 7.2.3 (bağımsız, yaklaşık ölçeklenmiş χ² bileşenler)
$$ \%90\ \mathrm{GA} = \left[\, e^{\hat d - t_{0{,}95;\,\nu}\,SE},\; e^{\hat d + t_{0{,}95;\,\nu}\,SE} \,\right] $$ :: EMA OIP Rev. 2 Bölüm 5.1 ölçüt 8 (log-normal varsayım)

## 4. Karar kuralı
$$ 0{,}85 \le e^{\hat d - t_{0{,}95;\,\nu}\,SE} \quad \text{ve} \quad e^{\hat d + t_{0{,}95;\,\nu}\,SE} \le 1{,}18 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{ölçüt sağlanır} $$ :: EMA OIP Rev. 2 Bölüm 5.1 ölçüt 8 (85–118); %90 GA’nın sınırlar içinde olması α = 0,05’te iki tek yönlü teste (TOST) denktir: Chow ve Liu (2008) §4.3.2; Casella ve Berger (2002) Alıştırma 8.47

Ekrandaki karar satırı (b) yoludur (FSR-TN-PLT-08 E-K3): Aşama 1 simülasyonunda (b) ve (c)’nin tip I hatası bütün tasarımlarda ≤ %5 iken (a) seri varyansı olduğunda nominali aşar (FSR-TN-PBE-01 Bölüm 11.7); (b) bu ikisinden daha yalın olanıdır. Ayrıca (b), bir varyans bileşeni sıfıra çekildiğinde SAS for Mixed Models’ın önerdiği analizin (NOBOUND ya da METHOD=TYPE3; Stroup ve ark. 2018 §5.7.2) dengeli tasarımdaki karşılığıdır; (c) ise varsayılan sınırlı REML ile Satterthwaite serbestlik derecesine karşılık gelir. Kitap bu sınırlı ve havuzlanmış analizin tip I hatayı şişirebileceğini belirtir (Stroup ve Littell 2002’ye atıfla); bizim simülasyonumuzda (c) nominali aşmadı. Diğer iki yolun kararı yanında gösterilir; yollar ayrışırsa belirtilir.

- Tek yönlü test EMA’da yoktur; log dönüşümü kutusu FDA’daki anlamıyla aynıdır.

# EMA 90% confidence interval: OIP Rev. 2, Section 5.1 criterion 8

## 1. Question and scope
Is the ratio of the geometric means of an in vitro measurement (e.g. the mass of a stage group of the aerodynamic particle size distribution) of test (T) and reference (R) within 0.85–1.18? Under Section 5.1 criterion 8 of the EMA guideline on inhaled products (CPMP/EWP/4151/00 Rev. 2, 2025) the comparison is presented as the 90% confidence interval (CI) of this ratio, assuming a log-normal distribution. The guideline does not define how the 90% CI is computed with batches and life stages; the platform shows three candidate paths together (KK-27). The computation uses the validated functions of the simulation engine (`invitro_array`, `ema_ci_fast`).

## 2. Model and notation
For each product ℓ batches, n units per batch and m life stages per unit are measured (balanced design). $X_{ijs}$: log measurement of unit i of batch j at stage s. The data-generating model is that of FSR-TN-PBE-01: fixed stage effect, random batch and unit effects, within-unit error.

- $\bar U_{ij}$: mean of the unit over the m stages; $\bar B_j$: mean of the unit means of the batch; $\bar g$: product mean.
- $\hat d = \bar g_T - \bar g_R$; point estimate $\mathrm{GMR} = e^{\hat d}$.
- $v_k$ and $\nu_k$: variance of the mean and degrees of freedom for product k; the three paths estimate these two differently.

## 3. Equations
Variance of the mean and degrees of freedom, three paths (k = T, R; the product index is omitted):

$$ s_U^2 = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{n} (\bar U_{ij} - \bar g)^2}{\ell n - 1}, \qquad v^{(a)} = \frac{s_U^2}{\ell n}, \qquad \nu^{(a)} = \ell n - 1 $$ :: FarmaStatR derivation (KK-27; FSR-TN-PBE-01 Section 5.3, path a)

Under the true model ($\sigma^2_B$ batch, $\sigma^2_C$ container, $\sigma^2_W$ within-container variance) the variance of the mean is $\operatorname{Var}(\bar g) = \sigma^2_B/\ell + \sigma^2_C/(\ell n) + \sigma^2_W/(\ell n m)$. The expectation of (a) contains the batch term only in the proportion $(\ell-1)/(\ell n - 1)$ (2/29 for 3 × 10); this is why the interval is too narrow when there is batch variance:

$$ E\left[v^{(a)}\right] = \frac{1}{\ell n}\left(\sigma^2_C + \frac{\sigma^2_W}{m} + \sigma^2_B\,\frac{n(\ell-1)}{\ell n - 1}\right) $$ :: Derived from Searle, Casella and McCulloch (2006) §3.5a, Eq. (50)–(51), Table 3.3 (FarmaStatR derivation)

$$ s_B^2 = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} (\bar B_j - \bar g)^2}{\ell - 1}, \qquad v^{(b)} = \frac{s_B^2}{\ell}, \qquad \nu^{(b)} = \ell - 1 $$ :: FarmaStatR derivation (KK-27; FSR-TN-PBE-01 Section 5.3, path b); distribution: (ℓ − 1)v^(b)/Var(ḡ) ~ χ²(ℓ − 1), Casella and Berger (2002) Theorem 5.3.1(c); in a balanced design equal to PROC MIXED NOBOUND or METHOD=TYPE3 (Stroup et al. 2018 §5.7.2; SAS check, FSR-LOG-01 G-105)

Path (c), mixed model with random batch and unit (REML). Mean squares of the three strata in a balanced design:

$$ MS_{\mathrm{batch}} = \frac{n m \sum_j (\bar B_j - \bar g)^2}{\ell - 1}, \qquad MS_{\mathrm{unit}} = \frac{m \sum_j \sum_i (\bar U_{ij} - \bar B_j)^2}{\ell (n - 1)} $$ :: Searle, Casella and McCulloch (2006) Chapter 4 (balanced design)
$$ MS_{\mathrm{res}} = \frac{\sum_j \sum_i \sum_s (X_{ijs} - \bar U_{ij} - \bar S_s + \bar g)^2}{(\ell n - 1)(m - 1)} $$ :: Searle, Casella and McCulloch (2006) Chapter 4 (balanced design)
$$ (\hat\theta_1, \hat\theta_2, \hat\theta_3) = \underset{\theta_1 \ge \theta_2 \ge \theta_3}{\mathrm{arg\,min}} \sum_{t=1}^{3} \mathrm{df}_t\,(MS_t - \theta_t)^2, \qquad v^{(c)} = \frac{\hat\theta_1}{\ell n m} $$ :: Searle, Casella and McCulloch (2006) Section 3.8 (REML for balanced data, constrained solution); extension to three strata: FarmaStatR derivation
$$ \nu^{(c)} = \text{sum of the df of the strata pooled with the batch stratum} $$ :: FarmaStatR derivation (FSR-TN-PBE-01 Section 5.3); with m = 1 equal to PROC MIXED bounded REML + DDFM=SATTERTHWAITE (SAS check, FSR-LOG-01 G-105)

Without pooling $v^{(c)} = MS_{\mathrm{batch}}/(\ell n m) = v^{(b)}$ and $\nu^{(c)} = \ell - 1$; path (c) equals path (b). $\bar S_s$ is the stage mean; with m = 1 there is no residual stratum. Confidence interval (for each path):

$$ SE = \sqrt{v_T + v_R}, \qquad \nu = \frac{(v_T + v_R)^2}{v_T^2/\nu_T + v_R^2/\nu_R} $$ :: Satterthwaite (1946); Casella and Berger (2002) Example 7.2.3 (independent, approximately scaled χ² components)
$$ 90\%\ \mathrm{CI} = \left[\, e^{\hat d - t_{0.95;\,\nu}\,SE},\; e^{\hat d + t_{0.95;\,\nu}\,SE} \,\right] $$ :: EMA OIP Rev. 2 Section 5.1 criterion 8 (log-normal assumption)

## 4. Decision rule
$$ 0.85 \le e^{\hat d - t_{0.95;\,\nu}\,SE} \quad \text{and} \quad e^{\hat d + t_{0.95;\,\nu}\,SE} \le 1.18 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{criterion met} $$ :: EMA OIP Rev. 2 Section 5.1 criterion 8 (85–118); the 90% CI within the limits is equivalent to two one-sided tests (TOST) at α = 0.05: Chow and Liu (2008) §4.3.2; Casella and Berger (2002) Exercise 8.47

The decision line on the screen is path (b) (FSR-TN-PLT-08 E-K3): in the Stage 1 simulation the type I error of (b) and (c) is ≤ 5% in all designs, whereas (a) exceeds the nominal level when there is batch variance (FSR-TN-PBE-01 Section 11.7); (b) is the simpler of the two. Moreover, (b) is the balanced-design counterpart of the analysis SAS for Mixed Models recommends when a variance component is set to zero (NOBOUND or METHOD=TYPE3; Stroup et al. 2018 §5.7.2), while (c) corresponds to the default bounded REML with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom. The book notes that this bounded, pooled analysis can inflate the type I error (citing Stroup and Littell 2002); in our simulation (c) did not exceed the nominal level. The decisions of the other two paths are shown alongside; if the paths differ, this is stated.

## 5. Implementation in the platform
- Choose “Method: EMA 90% CI” on the screen. The data format and input checks are those of FDA PBE (`pbe_kopru.R`): repeated or missing stages and different stage sets are rejected.
- Only balanced designs are computed (same number of containers in every batch); an unbalanced file gives a clear message, FDA PBE can be chosen.
- The computation uses `invitro_array` and `ema_ci_fast` (FSR-VAL-SIM-1 v1.1: TC-F3, TC-F4, TC-F11; pinned with SHA-256). `platform_ema` only calls them.
- If the degrees of freedom or the standard error of a path are zero (e.g. one batch), that path is shown as “could not be computed”; if (b) cannot be computed, no decision is given.
- There is no one-sided test in EMA; the log transform box has the same meaning as in FDA.
- SAS cross-checks: m = 1 PSG data and constructed data with a negative batch-variance estimate; (b) and (c) equal PROC MIXED to ten digits (FSR-LOG-01 G-103, G-105; programs in `belgeler/teknik_notlar/FSR-TN-PLT-08_ek/sas/`).

## 6. Worked example
The simulated data of PSG 020929 Appendix B (3 batches × 10 containers × 3 stages; the values given are taken as log scale):

- GMR = [[ema_gmr|1.1603]].
- (a): 90% CI [[ema_a_lo|0.9604]] – [[ema_a_hi|1.4017]], ν = [[ema_a_df|56.78]] → not met.
- (b): SE = [[ema_b_se|0.1309]], ν = [[ema_b_df|3.97]], 90% CI [[ema_b_lo|0.8772]] – [[ema_b_hi|1.5347]] → not met.
- (c): no pooling, hence the same as (b).

On the same data FDA PBE is established ($H_\eta$ = −0.031499). The difference is instructive: PBE weighs the mean difference together with the total variability; EMA looks only at the ratio of means, and path (b), which counts 3 batches rather than 30 containers as independent information, gives a wide interval. Reproduce it on the screen with “Example: FDA PSG 020929” and “Method: EMA 90% CI”.

## 7. Limitations
- The three paths are candidate implementations (FarmaStatR derivation, KK-27); none is defined in the guideline.
- EMA asks for at least three batches and at least ten units per batch; more with high variability (Rev. 2 Section 5.1). Smaller designs are computed with a warning.
- The analysis is for one quantity; criterion 8 requires all stage groups to pass (each group is analysed separately; one group failing may be acceptable in exceptional cases).
- For research and education; not a system validated for GxP environments.
